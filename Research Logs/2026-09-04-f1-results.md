# F1 results: tightening has a threshold, not a winner

Date: 2026-09-04. Run: `f1_seed_sweep.py`, 35 paired cells (7 domains x 5 seeds), 912 s.
Pre-registration: `Research Logs/2026-09-04-f1-preregistration.md` (committed `1a03da2`,
before the run). Analysis: `f1_analysis.py` (committed `91ca611`, before results existed).
Raw: `results/f1_seed_sweep.csv`, `results/f1_seed_sweep_cells.csv`.

## Gates

| gate | result |
|---|---|
| 1 -- seed 0 reproduces both saved notebook CSVs | **14,784 values exact** (11 cols x 96 (arm,z,budget) x 7 domains x 2 arms) |
| 2 -- null cells identical between arms | held in all 10 |
| one-match-many-`z` shortcut | re-verified per domain, 7/7 |
| retries | 0 in all 35 cells, so `base51` is a genuine v2 replication at every seed |

## The pre-registered test

Sign-flip permutation of Spearman rho between `area_ratio` and the paired delta in
`recall_at_budget`, z=1.0, K=250, `chromatin_od`:

* **primary (35 cells): rho = +0.5986, p = 0.0012**
* **decisive, seed 0 excluded (28 cells): rho = +0.5569, p = 0.0119**

Both below 0.05, so by the pre-registered decision rule **H1 is supported**: the effect is
monotone in template area.

## What that actually means -- a crossover, not a winner

A positive rho means the delta *rises* with template size. Split at the midpoint:

| `area_ratio` | tightened | n | mean delta | signs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **< 0.50** | <= 35 px | 14 | **-0.0360** | 13 neg / 1 pos |
| **>= 0.50** | >= 37 px | 11 | **+0.0048** | 4 neg / 5 pos / 2 zero |
| >= 0.58 | >= 39 px | 7 | +0.0097 | 1 neg / 4 pos / 2 zero |

**The relationship crosses zero at `area_ratio` ~ 0.50, i.e. a tightened template around
36 px.** Below it, largest-CC tightening costs 2-8 recall points. At or above it, the two
arms are indistinguishable.

**The pooled mean of -0.0129 is a property of this seed draw, not of the method.** 18 of the
25 informative cells landed at `area_ratio <= 0.53`, because that is what the click
distribution produced. A pool yielding mostly mild tightening would flip the aggregate's sign.
Reporting "base51 wins on average" would be reporting the sample.

## Effect size, stated correctly

`|mean delta|` against **that domain's own** `base51` seed SD:

| domain | seed SD | mean delta | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| human melanoma | 0.0066 | -0.0261 | **3.92x** |
| human breast cancer | 0.0110 | -0.0346 | **3.13x** |
| human neuroendocrine tumor | 0.0643 | -0.0442 | 0.69x |
| canine soft tissue sarcoma | 0.0064 | -0.0031 | 0.48x |
| canine lymphosarcoma | 0.0286 | +0.0122 | 0.43x |
| canine cutaneous mast cell tumor | 0.0453 | +0.0055 | 0.12x |
| canine lung cancer | 0.0322 | 0.0000 | 0 |

So "the effect is smaller than seed noise" is **false in 2 of 7 domains**. The pooled ratio
(0.42) is dragged down by the two domains with the largest seed SD and near-zero effect, and
it compares a *paired* effect against *unpaired* variability -- different questions. Paired,
the location shift is resolvable: mean -0.0129, SD 0.0275, **t = -2.77, sign-flip p = 0.0083,
Wilcoxon p = 0.0101**.

This run therefore landed in the **first** row of the pre-registered decision table, not the
third. "Not a decision-relevant lever" was the wrong reading; the lever exists and has a
threshold.

## Robustness

| subset | n | rho | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| all cells (pre-registered primary) | 35 | +0.5986 | 0.0001 |
| informative only (nulls dropped) | 25 | +0.5591 | 0.0037 |
| all zero deltas dropped | 23 | +0.5157 | 0.0118 |
| nulls + seed 0 dropped | 19 | +0.4537 | 0.0510 |
| **all zero deltas + seed 0 dropped** | 17 | +0.3619 | **0.1535** |

The dose-response is **not** manufactured by the null cells anchoring `area_ratio = 1.0`
(informative-only survives at p = 0.0037). But the strictest subset -- dropping every zero
delta *and* the seed-0 cells that suggested the hypothesis -- does not clear 0.05 at n=17.
The pre-registration committed to keeping nulls with a stated rationale, so this is a caveat
rather than a violation; it belongs next to the headline.

## Axis specificity: direction replicates, dose does not

* `tm_score`: mean delta **-0.0153** (slightly larger than `chromatin_od`'s -0.0129),
  14 neg / 7 pos, cross-axis per-cell delta correlation **0.35**.
* `tm_score` dose-response: rho = +0.164 (p = 0.399); seed-0-excluded rho = -0.011 (p = 0.962).

So *tightening hurts on both rankers*; only the *monotone structure* is specific to
`chromatin_od`, the ranker that can see chromatin density.

## Cost side

`largest_cc`'s list is longer in every informative cell at z >= 1.0 (mean **+2,751**
detections). At z=1.0 its `read_95` is deeper -- worse -- in **20 of 25** informative cells,
median **+355**.

The one place it looks better is `read_95` *censoring*: across the z grid `base51` fails to
reach 95% recall in 36 (arm, z) cells against `largest_cc`'s 20. But there is **no censoring
at all at z=1.0** (0 vs 0); the whole asymmetry sits at z=2.5 (6 vs 2) and z=3.0 (12 vs 8),
where the longer list is what reaches deeper. It is list length, not ranking quality.

## Conclusion

**The useful result is a threshold, not a winner: do not tighten below ~36 px.**

Above that, largest-CC tightening is indistinguishable from the fixed 51 px box on the metric
`compare.py` designates primary, while costing ~20-30% more candidates to read. Below it, it
costs real recall -- 3-4x the within-arm seed SD in human melanoma and human breast cancer.

This settles what the largest-CC notebook's deliverable (d) could not. Its own first verdict
bullet -- *"recall unchanged at the operating point, strictly better beyond it"* -- was
measured on `full_list_recall` at coverage 0.90-0.999, where both arms return 1.0 and the
metric discriminates nothing (audit Findings 1 and 2). Its third bullet, *"reading depth:
mixed, no consistent direction, no domain property visible that predicts the sign"*, was the
honest reading of one seed: the sign is not domain-predicted, it is **dose**-predicted, and
one seed per domain cannot see that.

## Corrections to commit `b81c230`'s message

Three statements in that commit are wrong or overstated, corrected here rather than by
rewriting history:

1. *"The smaller the tightened template, the worse the ranking"* -- directionally right but it
   omits the crossover; at `area_ratio >= 0.5` the arms are indistinguishable.
2. *"Real, ordered, and smaller than seed noise"* -- false in 2 of 7 domains (3.9x and 3.1x),
   and it compares a paired effect to unpaired variability. The paired location shift is
   resolvable at p = 0.0083.
3. *"the exploratory slope grid is identical across every z on the tm_score axis"* -- false in
   2 of 48 cells (at K=5000, z=2.5 gives -0.289 and z=3.0 gives -0.556, against -0.253
   elsewhere). The robust statement is that `chromatin_od`'s slope is positive in 47 of 48
   grid cells.

## Follow-ups

* The threshold (~36 px) is estimated from 25 informative cells with a crossover located by
  eye at the midpoint. Locating it properly wants either more seeds or a deliberate sweep of
  `base_size` at fixed seed -- the latter is cheaper and removes the seed draw entirely as a
  source of dose variation.
* `f1_analysis.py`'s docstring says it was "written and committed before the run's results
  were inspected". True of `91ca611`; it was then modified inside `b81c230` to fix the
  `pivot_table(dropna=False)` cartesian-product bug. The fix is benign and every published
  number reproduces with it, but the docstring should say so.
